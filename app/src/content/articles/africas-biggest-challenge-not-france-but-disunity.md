I have been following the conversations around the visit of Emmanuel Macron to Nairobi and his participation in the Africa Forward Summit this week. A lot of the commentary has been centered on the idea that France is losing influence in parts of West Africa and is now turning its attention toward East Africa. Maybe there is some truth in that, maybe not. It is true that countries like Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger have changed the nature of their relationship with France in recent years. But I also think Africans sometimes spend too much time blaming foreign powers without asking ourselves hard questions about our own leadership and direction as a continent.

Every powerful country pursues its interests. France does it, China does it. The United States and Russia do the same. That is how international politics works. The bigger question is; do African countries have a clear and common vision for themselves? One thing Europe understands very well is unity around strategic interests. Even when European countries disagree internally, they usually know when to stand together. You often see this at the United Nations or during major global negotiations. Africa, unfortunately, still struggles with that. We have institutions like the African Union and the East African Community, but when serious decisions come up, national interests tend to outweigh continental priorities. Even economically, African countries still trade more with Europe, China and other foreign markets than with each other despite the existence of the African Continental Free Trade Area, that alone says a lot.

At some point, we need to stop acting as if Africa is only a victim of external influence. Foreign powers will always pursue opportunities where they exist. The real issue is whether African leaders are capable of defining and defending African interests with one voice. Maybe the debate should not just be about France moving from West Africa to East Africa. Maybe the real conversation should be about whether Africa itself is finally ready to think and act strategically as one continent.

Evidently prices for consumer goods have spiked. Recently, I visited a restaurant in Central Business District for a cup of tea, to my dismay, upon paying I realized that I had paid twice the amount for the same cup of tea a few weeks ago. When I expressed my disbelief, the waitress made the sentiments that the price of sugar and cost of living as risen. It is beyond no doubt that standards of livings have gone higher than pre-pandemic times. I recently parted ways with Rwf 15,000 for a purchase of five litre jerrycan of cooking that used to retail for Rwf 10,000. The whole region is characterized by fuel shortage. The cost of charcoal and cooking gas amidst limited supply has been rising at alarming rate. A kilogramme of charcoal that went for Rwf 250 now costs Rwf 600 and the cost of a 6 kg gas cylinder that was Rwf 5000 is now Rwf 7560. This seems to be a predicament shared by most if not all African countries. Enter wheat prices!

Africa is currently feeling the most significant shockwaves of the Russia-Ukraine war due to its heavy reliance on imported wheat. The war has exposed vulnerabilities of our major reliance on imported food. Thirty percent of Africa's wheat consumption comes from Russia and Ukraine. Specifically, in Egypt, the two countries (Russia and Ukraine) account for 80% of its wheat import. Global shortage of wheat has led to a sharp price hike, this signals a looming crisis such as civil unrest for low income countries where most of the African nations lie. Previously price hikes of staple food such as bread have led to riots in countries such as Egypt and event triggered revolution such as one that led to the ouster of the long time dictator in Sudan; Omar al Bashir. Moreover, Russia is also a major supplier of fertilizer; a critical agro input for agrarian countries that we are.

Africa is characterized as land abundant (52% of the world's arable land is located in the continent) therefore land endowment is not a constraint for agricultural development. About two thirds of this continent's work force is employed in the agricultural sector and the sector contributes to between 30 to 60 percent of each country's GDP. It is therefore paradoxical that we are net food importers. Africa Development Bank (AfDB) reports that the continent's food imports include wheat, sugar, rice, beef, and soybeans; commodities that can be produced on the continent. Our economic prosperity and the agricultural sector's performance are inextricably linked, yet our reliance on nature is a source of vulnerability and the agricultural sector is susceptible to shocks. It is a stark reality that Africa is facing increasing threat of climate change on health, food and water security. Climate change has had a catastrophic impact on agriculture engendering plummeting of food availability and access owing to food production shortage. In the recent times, the continent has experienced devastating floods in South Africa, desert locust invasion in Ethiopia and a drought in South Sudan. All these factors portend looming far-reaching and lasting consequences on a continent already facing acute hunger.

The 3rd Comprehensive African Agriculture Development Programme (CAADP) Biennial Review Report launched in 2022, asserted that African countries are severely off track in meeting the objectives of The June 2014 Malabo Declaration on Accelerated Agricultural Growth and Transformation for Shared Prosperity and Improved Livelihoods that mobilizes and harmonizes the continents effort towards making agriculture a priority. The assessment revealed that only one country (Rwanda) is on track in meeting the 7 commitments of the declaration which includes among them allocating 10% of public funds to agriculture. Kenya has allocated only 3% of it GDP to the sector.

Responding to rising food concerns, in a bid to thwart inflation and protect local food supplies, countries will apply food protectionist policies, further worsening food insecurity. Africa must therefore look within its food basket to feed its people. Bold steps need to be undertaken by our leaders to expedite much-needed transformation to harness the potential of our natural endowment. Urgent concerted continental effort and coalesced commitment to meeting the objectives of the June 2014 Malabo Declaration is essential.

Our leaders' staggering commitment to invest in agriculture and implementation of ineffective policy in agriculture, trade and investment is threat to the socio-economic development of the continent. We have been unable to adopt farming systems and policies to shape an economic system to suit our continent's natural resources. Upset of the eco-system such as over-extraction from natural water resources is leading to drought and unsustainable agricultural practices to soil erosion. Small holder farmers who dominate the continent's agricultural production are the most poor and government support in terms of policy reforms and financial incentives for them is lacking.

Agri-food systems transformation is imperative in Africa. Governments in the continent should create an enabling environment for the agricultural sector to thrive, especially catalyzing increased agricultural production and productivity to achieve nutrition and food security; enhancing performances of agricultural value chains and agribusiness. All this we can achieve by engaging small holder and capacitating in initiatives such as climate-smart agriculture and improving post-harvest handling (to reduce waste of valuable food, land, water, labour and inputs utilized to produce) for sustainability in the continent.

More importantly, African countries should capitalize on AfCFTA in promoting trade of agricultural and processed agricultural produce. Currently, Intra-African trade in agriculture lies below 20%. The agreement has the potential of lifting millions of Africans out of poverty and end food insecurity, it will do so by creating the world's largest free trading area; removing tariff and non-tariff barriers on goods and products. African countries should take advantage of The Framework for Boosting Intra-African Trade in Agricultural Commodities and Services launched to boost Intra-African trade in agriculture.

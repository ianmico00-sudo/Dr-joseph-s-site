Leadership advice is everywhere. We are constantly told that leaders must be confident, decisive, and able to inspire others. Yet when we observe situations where local leaders struggle to deliver results, the problem is rarely about lack of ambition or good intentions. In many cases, the real challenge emerges in how leaders respond when they face pressure. Leadership is not only tested when things are moving smoothly. It is tested when decisions are difficult, when responsibility cannot be shifted, and when expectations from citizens are high.

From what I have observed in governance spaces, many local leaders genuinely want to perform well. They understand their responsibilities and they are committed to improving service delivery. However, hesitation to take decisions, fear of being blamed if something goes wrong, and sometimes the tendency to wait for direction from above can slow down implementation. Over time, this affects the speed at which services reach citizens, even when policies and decentralization frameworks are already in place.

Leaders who stand out are those who remain focused on solving problems rather than avoiding them. They are willing to take responsibility, consult where necessary, and move forward even when conditions are not perfect. They do not see feedback as criticism, but as useful information that helps them adjust and improve. In practice, citizens do not only evaluate leaders based on plans or speeches. They look at whether issues raised are addressed, whether services improve, and whether leaders show ownership of the mandates entrusted to them.
